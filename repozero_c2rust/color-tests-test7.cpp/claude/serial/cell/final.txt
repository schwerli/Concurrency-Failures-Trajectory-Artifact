## What the black-box probing established

**`blend` is `a*(1-ratio) + b*ratio` in `f32`**, with no clamping of colors or ratio. The associativity is not cosmetic — the algebraically identical `a + (b-a)*ratio` disagrees by 1 ULP, normally invisible under 6-digit printing but exposed when the terms cancel: `1 0 0 -9 0 0.1` must print `-5.96046e-08`, not `0`. That case also ruled out `f64` math, which would give `-1.48601e-08`.

**Two argument-handling quirks in the C++**, both preserved: `b2` is declared `0.1f` and never read from `argv`, so the 6th argument is the *ratio* and a 7th is silently ignored — despite the example inputs naming their 6th key `b2` and 7th `ratio`. And `argc > 5` means all five values parse together or not at all (`./test7 1 2 3 abc` never throws).

**Output is `printf("%g")` with 6 significant digits**, plus glibc's `nan`/`-nan`/`inf` spellings — Rust's native float formatting matches on none of these.

**`std::stof` needed a real `strtof`**: whitespace skipping, tail-ignoring (`0.5xyz` → `0.5`), hex, `infinity`, `nan(123)`. Its range rules are asymmetric, which is where the fuzzer earned its keep — it caught that `1.1754943e-38` must throw `out_of_range` even though it correctly rounds to `FLT_MIN`. glibc detects underflow by IEEE tininess *after rounding to 24 bits with an unbounded exponent*, not by asking whether the result is subnormal; the neighbouring `1.17549435e-38` is accepted. Overflow, conversely, is judged on the rounded result. Uncaught exceptions reproduce the `terminate called after throwing…` stderr text and SIGABRT (status 134).
