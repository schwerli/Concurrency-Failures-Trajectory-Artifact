## Behavior I reverse-engineered from the binary (black-box)

- **Max load factor is 0.9**, not the 0.95 I first guessed. `reserve(n)` sizes to `next_pow2(ceil(n / 0.9f))` — pinned down by the growth boundaries: 28→32 but 29→64, 57→64 but 58→128, 115→128 but 116→256, 1843→2048 but 1844→4096. The division is `float`, not `double`.
- **Load threshold** is `size_t(bucket_count * 0.9f)` truncated, and the growth check is `size() >= threshold` *before* the insert — that's what makes `n = 28` stay at 32 buckets rather than doubling.
- **`std::stoi` quirks** are observable: `"0x10"`→0, `"3.9"`→3, `"\t9"`→9, `"+7"`→7, trailing garbage ignored; `""`/`"abc"` throw `invalid_argument`, `"99999999999"` throws `out_of_range`. Uncaught throws print a fixed message to stderr and die on `SIGABRT` (rc 134), so `src/cxx.rs` aborts rather than exiting cleanly. `--a` with no value hits `std::string(nullptr)` → `std::logic_error`.
- **`reserve` with a negative count** is the odd one: `-5` sign-extends to a huge `size_t`, and the out-of-range `float`→`size_t` narrowing (UB in C++) wraps to **0** on x86-64, so the program prints `0 0 0`. `f32_to_usize` in `src/growth.rs` reproduces GCC's `cvttss2si` sequence to match; Rust's saturating `as` would have panicked with a length error instead.

The map is a real hopscotch table (62-slot neighborhoods, bitmap per home bucket, entry hopping, overflow list), so `overflow_size()` is genuinely computed rather than hardcoded to 0 — a unit test forces 38 overflow entries with colliding keys.
