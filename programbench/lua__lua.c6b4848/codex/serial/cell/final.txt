The workspace now contains a clean-room reimplementation: [cleanlua.py](/workspace/cleanlua.py) holds a self-contained lexer, parser, runtime, REPL, package loader, and standard-library surface, [executable](/workspace/executable) is the final launcher, and [compile.sh](/workspace/compile.sh) verifies the source and restores executable permissions. `./executable` no longer relies on the provided binary.

I verified the final path with `./compile.sh`, `./executable -v`, `./executable -e 'print(1+2)'`, `printf 'print(2+3)\n' | ./executable -i`, plus a larger set of differential checks against the oracle during development covering option parsing, env init, `arg`, script/stdin execution, REPL prompts, stack traces, `require`, warnings, metatables, numeric behavior, and representative library calls. Residual risk is the usual one for a language runtime: I did not exhaustively diff every Lua edge case.
